## What it is

The International Tea Room (ITR) is a **community seminar series** for researchers working
with gastruloids and other stem cell-based embryo models. Sessions are informal by design:
the goal is to discuss work in progress, preprints and unpublished data, and to compare notes
on the details of culture methods and analysis that usually stay out of published papers.

## Who it is for

Anyone actively working with, or planning to work with, stem cell-based embryo models:

- gastruloids (mouse, human and other species)
- trunk-like structures, somitoids, neuruloids and other regionalised models
- blastoids, embryoids, ETX/ETiX and post-implantation models
- micropattern-based 2D models and organ-level assembloids

Students, postdocs and group leaders are equally welcome. Membership is by request so that
the group stays a working community rather than a broadcast list. See [Join]({{< relref "subscribe" >}}).

## Format

- **When:** second Monday of every month, 14:00 UK time (GMT/BST), October to July
- **Where:** Zoom (link sent to members by email before each session)
- **Length:** 50–60 minutes; roughly 30–40 minutes of presentation followed by open discussion
- **Session types:**
  - *Group leader sessions* — one speaker, one longer talk
  - *Early-career sessions* — two shorter talks by PhD students or postdocs
  - *Occasional roundtables* on methods, reproducibility and emerging trends
- **Recordings:** where the speaker agrees, sessions are recorded and screened for members in
  time zones that cannot join live (Americas and Asia–Pacific).

## Community

Beyond the monthly sessions, members can join a **Slack workspace** for follow-up questions to
speakers, methods troubleshooting, and announcements of positions, preprints and meetings.
Slack access is invite-only; tick the box on the [join form]({{< relref "subscribe" >}}).

## History

The series began as the *Gastruloids Tea Room* — informal meetings hosted by Alfonso Martinez
Arias' lab in the tea room of the Department of Genetics, University of Cambridge. During the
COVID-19 pandemic it moved to Zoom and opened to labs worldwide. In 2026 the organising team
expanded, and with the 2026–27 season the series widened its scope to all stem cell-based
embryo models.
